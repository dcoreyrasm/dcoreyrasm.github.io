Virtual AI Training Academy

A Multi-Day AI Academy for the Professionals You Serve

Sample Agenda

A three-day academy, session by session.

This sample agenda was built for workforce development professionals. Each session is adapted from a live class or workshop I already teach, and I tailor the scenarios and examples to your audience.

Day 1: Build a strong foundation

Session 1 · Day 1

Practical AI for Workforce Professionals

This opening session gives everyone a shared starting point. Using workforce scenarios from case management, business services, and job seeker support, I'll show where AI can save time on routine tasks, where it gets things wrong, and what customer information should never go into a public AI tool. Participants will also see how they can help job seekers use AI safely.

Participants will be able to

  • Identify routine workforce tasks where AI can save time and tasks where it shouldn't be used.
  • Explain in plain language why AI answers can be wrong and need a human check.
  • Name the types of customer information that should never go into a public AI tool.

Take-home: An AI starter guide with privacy settings for four free tools.

Session 2 · Day 1

Getting Started and Having a Real Conversation with AI

This hands-on session compares ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot side by side. Participants will check the privacy settings that control what happens to their chats, practice a simple way to ask for what they need, and use follow-up questions to shape an answer instead of starting over. We'll also cover how to share a document safely and how to tell when an answer needs a second look.

Participants will be able to

  • Find and adjust the privacy settings in at least two free AI tools.
  • Give AI the details it needs, such as who it's for, the tone, and the length, and use follow-up questions to improve the result.
  • Decide what is safe to share in a file, a photo, or a voice chat.

Take-home: A prompt set for getting better answers with follow-up questions.

Session 3 · Day 1

Putting AI to Work on Everyday Workforce Tasks

This session puts AI to work on the daily tasks that eat up time. Participants will write emails and announcements in their own voice, turn a long document such as a benefits packet or program guide into plain language, and plan a busy week. We'll also look at how Copilot works inside Word, Excel, and Outlook, and what should never be pasted into a document or email.

Participants will be able to

  • Draft and edit workforce communications in their own voice with AI.
  • Turn a long or complex document into plain language for job seekers or employers.
  • Keep personal and financial details out of prompts, files, and email.

Take-home: A plain-language rewrite guide and a set of everyday workforce prompts.

Day 2: Serve job seekers and learners

Session 4 · Day 2

Your Research Assistant: Building a Notebook from Your Own Program Documents

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) answers questions from the sources you give it, and it points back to where each answer came from. Participants will build a notebook from program guides, policy manuals, and local labor market reports, then use it to answer questions, spot trends, and create study guides and audio overviews for staff and job seekers.

Participants will be able to

  • Build a notebook from their own program documents and labor market reports.
  • Get answers that point back to their sources and check them before using them.
  • Decide what to keep out of a notebook and who can see it when it's shared.

Take-home: A notebook setup guide for working from your own documents.

Session 5 · Day 2

AI for Adult Learners and Job Seekers

Job seekers are already using AI, and employers are using it to screen applicants. This session prepares staff to coach job seekers through using AI to prepare for a job search, including resumes, cover letters, and interview practice. Participants will practice checking what AI produces for accuracy and honesty, and they'll learn simple ways to help job seekers protect their personal information.

Participants will be able to

  • Coach a job seeker through using AI for a resume and interview practice while keeping it accurate and honest.
  • Check AI-generated job search materials for errors, exaggeration, and bias.
  • Teach job seekers what personal information to keep out of AI tools.

Take-home: A job seeker coaching guide with prompts staff can share.

Session 6 · Day 2

No Laptop Required: Opening AI Access for the Learners Most Likely to Be Left Out

The learners who most need a path into good jobs often have the least access to AI tools, training, and support. Using learner scenarios, such as a single parent with only a phone or an older worker returning after a layoff, participants will map barriers like devices, cost, confidence, trust, and time. Then they'll review AI examples for accuracy, bias, and privacy risk and plan one practical change for their own program.

Participants will be able to

  • Identify barriers to AI access, including connectivity, cost, confidence, trust, and time.
  • Evaluate AI-generated material for accuracy, bias, and privacy risk.
  • Create a 30-day AI access plan using free or low-cost resources and community partners.

Take-home: A Learner Barrier Map and a 30-day AI access plan template.

Day 3: Lead the change in your organization

Session 7 · Day 3

From Experimentation to Practice: How AI Governance Takes Shape in Organizations

Many workforce organizations are using AI before they have shared guidance for it. This session looks at how responsible AI practices develop as teams move from informal experimentation toward shared expectations. Participants will work through practical scenarios involving privacy, review and approval, unequal access to tools, and responsible experimentation, and they'll draft a basic decision guide for their organization.

Participants will be able to

  • Distinguish AI governance policies from everyday AI practices.
  • Identify common decision points for responsible AI experimentation and approval.
  • Draft a basic decision guide for AI use, review, and escalation.

Take-home: An AI decision guide template for use, review, and escalation.

Session 8 · Day 3

Making AI Work for You, Safely: Your 30-Day AI Plan

The closing session brings the academy together. Participants will learn how to set up AI memory and preferences on their own terms, understand connected apps before turning them on, and know what it means when AI can act on their computer. We'll also cover how to spot made-up facts and AI scams. Each participant will finish with a personal safe-use checklist and a 30-day AI plan with a goal, an owner, and a review step.

Participants will be able to

  • Review and change what an AI tool remembers, and evaluate connected apps before turning them on.
  • Explain what AI assistants that can act on a computer can see and do, and how to stay safe.
  • Build a 30-day AI plan with a goal, an owner, and a review step.

Take-home: A safe-use checklist and a 30-day AI plan template.

Make It Yours

Built around your audience and schedule.

  • Choose your sessions. Swap in topics from my classes and workshops, such as AI policy, change management, or a specific tool.
  • Choose your schedule. Run it over three days, as a weekly series, or as a single half-day intensive.
  • Match your field. I adapt the scenarios to the work your participants do every day and can map sessions to the competency framework your organization uses.
  • Keep it practical. The content is informational and tool-neutral, with no product pitches. Participants practice with free tools.

Questions

Good to know.

Do participants need paid AI tools?
No. Everyone practices with free versions of common AI tools. When a paid feature comes up, I'll point it out and show a free alternative where one exists.
Is it accessible?
Yes. I build slides and handouts to meet WCAG 2.1 AA guidelines, I ask that live captions be turned on, and I can share materials in advance for participants who use assistive technology.
Can the sessions be recorded?
Yes, in most cases. We'll agree on recording and material reuse terms in the written agreement.
How is it priced?
The academy is priced as one package that covers curriculum design, live delivery, and all participant materials. I'll send a written proposal with the scope, the deliverables, and a fixed fee.

Every one of these tools keeps an allowance behind the scenes, and most of us only find out when a message says we are out. This week is about looking before you get there. Each tool shows it in a different place, and one of them shows very little. As always, AI can sound sure and still be wrong, so check anything about money, health, or safety against a real source, and keep private details out of any tool you do not fully trust. Limits change, so treat the numbers on these pages as a snapshot.

## Claude: see How Much of Your Plan You Have Used

![Claude: See How Much of Your Plan You Have Used](/assets/blog/2026-11-01/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude has a usage page that shows where you stand in your current session and for the week.

1. Open Settings, then select Usage.
2. Look at Plan usage limits at the top. Current session shows how much of your five-hour session you have used and how much time is left.
3. Check Weekly limits to see when your weekly limit resets.
4. To make your allowance last, put related questions in one message, and keep documents you reuse in a project.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it before a big writing or research session, so you know whether to start now or wait. Boundary: Anthropic's page lists the usage bars for Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans. It does not say whether Free plans see them, so I cannot verify that.

Source: [Anthropic Help Center, Usage limit best practices](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9797557-usage-limit-best-practices)

## ChatGPT: read the Reset Time When You Hit a Limit

![ChatGPT: Read the Reset Time When You Hit a Limit](/assets/blog/2026-11-01/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

I could not find a screen in ChatGPT's help pages that shows what you have left. What it does show is when an allowance resets, so that is the thing to look for.

1. Use ChatGPT as you normally would. There is nothing to turn on.
2. If you reach a limit, look for the reset time in the message. OpenAI says ChatGPT displays when an allowance resets when that information is available.
3. If it was a Thinking limit, check which model is answering next. OpenAI says ChatGPT may continue with another available model.
4. Do not wait on support to fix it. OpenAI says its Support team does not reset ChatGPT usage limits, and reaching an allowance does not by itself mean your account was restricted.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when a hard question suddenly gets a different kind of answer and you want to know why. Boundary: the page says Free and Go have unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards, but uploads and image generation have separate limits. It describes no running meter of what you have left. As of October 2026.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center, GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Pro in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001354-gpt-56-and-gpt-6-pro-in-chatgpt)

## Copilot: check Your AI Credits in Your Microsoft Account

![Copilot: Check Your AI Credits in Your Microsoft Account](/assets/blog/2026-11-01/copilot.png){: .section-image }

This one is the Microsoft 365 subscription side of Copilot, not the free Copilot app. On Personal and Family plans, AI features in apps like Word and Excel draw from a monthly pool of credits.

1. Sign in to your Microsoft account in a web browser.
2. Select Services & subscriptions.
3. Look for the status of your limits and AI credits. Microsoft says Personal and Family each include 60 AI credits per month.
4. If you are on a Family plan, check with the person who owns the subscription. Microsoft says AI benefits are only available to the subscription owner and cannot be shared.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when Copilot inside Word or Excel says you are out, but you are not sure why. Boundary: Microsoft's page gives only that one path, says the credits are monthly without saying when they reset, and says you will be notified when you reach a limit. Premium is described as extensive usage beyond the standard credit limits.

Source: [Microsoft Support, AI credits and limits for Microsoft 365 subscriptions](https://support.microsoft.com/office/68530f1a-4459-4d02-9818-8233c1f673b8)

## Gemini: open the Usage Limits Page

![Gemini: Open the Usage Limits Page](/assets/blog/2026-11-01/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Gemini has a dedicated Usage limits page in its settings, which makes it the easiest of the four to check.

1. Go to gemini.google.com and sign in with your personal Google Account.
2. At the bottom left, select Settings, then select Usage limits.
3. Read how you are doing against your limits. Google says there is a five-hour limit that refreshes on a rolling basis, and a weekly limit as well.
4. Keep an eye out for the notices. Google says Gemini tells you when you are close to a limit and when it refreshes.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it before a long, heavy session. Google says longer chats, advanced models, and higher thinking levels use more of your usage. Boundary: the page covers personal Google Accounts only, work or school accounts are described separately, and Google says limits may change without notice.

Source: [Gemini Apps Help, Gemini Apps limits and upgrades for Google AI subscribers](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805?hl=en)

## Try this

Open the usage page in the tool you use most and write down two things: when your current allowance resets, and which of your habits eats the most of it, like long chats, uploads, or images. Then change one habit this week, such as putting five small questions into one message, and see if you notice the difference. If you use a work or school account, check with your admin, since the pages I read cover personal accounts.

There's a particular kind of forgetting that comes with AI. You get a good answer, then lose it in a pile of old chats. You mean to write an errand down, and don't. Or you trust something that was true last month and isn't anymore. This issue is four small ways to fix that, one per tool. None of it replaces double-checking anything that involves money, health, or safety, but for the everyday stuff you're tired of carrying around in your head, it helps.

## Claude: location-Based Suggestions

![Claude: Location-Based Suggestions](/assets/blog/2026-09-23/claude.png){: .section-image }

On iPhone or iPad, Claude can use your location to suggest real places nearby, dinner spots, coffee shops, whatever you ask for, right inside the chat.

1. Open a chat with Claude on your iPhone or iPad and allow Location access when it asks.
2. Ask something concrete, like "What's nearby for dinner?" or "Any good coffee shops near me?"
3. Look over Claude's suggestions on the interactive map it shows you.
4. Tap a spot to open it in Apple Maps for directions.

**When to reach for it:** it only works on iPhone and iPad, and Claude says outright that people on Team or Enterprise plans can't use the location tool yet.

Source: [Use Claude with iOS apps, Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11869619-use-claude-with-ios-apps)

## ChatGPT: search Your Chat History

![ChatGPT: Search Your Chat History](/assets/blog/2026-09-23/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

If you already asked ChatGPT something and worked out an answer, you don't have to re-explain yourself. You can just find that conversation again.

1. Open ChatGPT on the web or the app, signed in.
2. Click the search icon in the sidebar, or press Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on a Mac).
3. Type a keyword from the conversation, like "buttermilk substitute" or "deductible."
4. Open the matching conversation instead of typing the whole question over again.

**When to reach for it:** it can only find what still exists. Delete a conversation and OpenAI says it comes out of the search index for good, so it's gone.

Source: [How do I search my chat history in ChatGPT?, OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10056348-how-do-i-search-my-chat-history-in-chatgpt)

## Copilot: switch To Search Mode

![Copilot: Switch To Search Mode](/assets/blog/2026-09-23/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Copilot's regular answers come from what it already knows, which can be out of date. Search mode makes it check the live web instead.

1. Open Copilot on the web, on Windows, or in the app, and type your question.
2. Tap the mode selector under the prompt box. It defaults to "Quick response."
3. Choose "Search."
4. Send your question. Copilot answers with current web results and sources instead of guessing.

**When to reach for it:** microsoft describes it as built for timely information, which is really an admission that the regular mode can be stale. Treat anything time-sensitive, hours, prices, whether something's still open, as worth a second look, not the final word.

Source: [Conversation modes in Microsoft Copilot, Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/conversation-modes-in-microsoft-copilot)

## Gemini: capture Tasks And Reminders

![Gemini: Capture Tasks And Reminders](/assets/blog/2026-09-23/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Instead of trying to remember an errand until you get home, just tell Gemini. It lands in a real task list, not a chat you'll never reopen.

1. Make sure you're signed into the Gemini app with Keep Activity turned on.
2. Say something like "Add butter and lemon to my grocery list" or "Remind me Thursday at 5pm to call the vet."
3. It saves straight to Google Tasks, or Samsung Reminder on supported Galaxy phones.
4. Ask "what's on my to-do list?" any time to have it read back to you.

**When to reach for it:** samsung Reminder only works on a Galaxy S25 or later running a recent OneUI version. And Google says plainly that Gemini can still get things wrong, so double check anything that actually matters.

Source: [Capture your tasks & reminders with Gemini Apps, Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15230285)

## Try this

Pick one small thing you're currently holding in your head, an errand you keep meaning to write down, an answer you already worked out with one of these tools, or a fact you're not fully sure is still true. Use the matching trick above to get it out of your head. See how much lighter that one thing feels once it's somewhere else.

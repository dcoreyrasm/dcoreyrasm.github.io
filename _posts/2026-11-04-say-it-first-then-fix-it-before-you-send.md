Some messages are easier to say than to type. This week is about dictation, where you talk and your words show up as text you can read and fix before anything goes out. It is different from a spoken back and forth with the AI. You stay in charge of what gets sent. One caution that applies to all four: speech to text mishears things, so read it before you hit send, and keep private details out of any tool you do not fully trust.

## Claude: dictate a prompt in the Claude mobile app

![Claude: Dictate a prompt in the Claude mobile app](/assets/blog/2026-11-04/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude on your phone can turn your speech into a text prompt. Claude then answers in text, not out loud.

1. Open the Claude app on iOS or Android and start a new chat.
2. Tap the microphone icon on the right side of the message box. The first time, pick your language.
3. Say what you want to ask or write.
4. Tap the arrow to send, or the X to cancel without sending.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when your thumbs are slower than your thoughts. It is on every plan, including Free. Anthropic says it deletes the audio after converting it to text and does not keep a copy or use it for training. It is not voice mode, so it will not hold a spoken conversation. Languages other than English are still in beta.

Source: [Anthropic Help Center, Use dictation on Claude Mobile](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10065434-use-dictation-on-claude-mobile)

## ChatGPT: dictate, edit the text, then send

![ChatGPT: Dictate, edit the text, then send](/assets/blog/2026-11-04/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

ChatGPT can turn your speech into text in the message box. You get to change the text before it goes anywhere.

1. Open a chat and select the microphone icon in the message box.
2. Speak. Your recording is sent to OpenAI's models to be transcribed.
3. Read the text that comes back and fix anything it got wrong. OpenAI says you can edit it before sending.
4. If you would rather not share the audio, open your data controls settings and turn off "Include your audio recordings."

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when you want to ramble out a first draft and clean it up in text. The catch is the audio. OpenAI says dictation audio is kept as long as that chat stays in your history, and deleting the chat removes it, usually within 30 days. That help page does not list which plans or devices have dictation, so check your own app.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center, Voice Dictation FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12168547-voice-dictation-faq)

## Copilot: dictate messages to Microsoft Copilot Chat

![Copilot: Dictate messages to Microsoft Copilot Chat](/assets/blog/2026-11-04/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Copilot Chat can write down what you say as you say it. This is the Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, which needs a work or school account, not the free personal Copilot app.

1. Go to m365.cloud.microsoft/chat or open the Microsoft Copilot desktop app, and sign in with your work or school account.
2. In the prompt box, select "Start dictation." Allow microphone access if it asks.
3. Speak naturally. Say "period," "comma," or "new line" to add punctuation.
4. Select "Stop dictation," then read the text and fix anything it misheard.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when you are already working in Microsoft 365 and want to talk through a request. Microsoft's page covers web and the desktop app only. It does not mention mobile, languages, or time limits, and I cannot verify those. Background noise will hurt accuracy.

Source: [Microsoft Support, Dictate messages to Microsoft Copilot Chat](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/dictate-messages-to-microsoft-365-copilot-chat)

## Gemini: talk to Type in Chrome, powered by Gemini

![Gemini: Talk to Type in Chrome, powered by Gemini](/assets/blog/2026-11-04/gemini.png){: .section-image }

This one is a Chrome feature that uses Gemini, so it is not inside the Gemini app. It turns your speech into cleaned-up text in almost any text box on the web, and you can tell it how to change what you already wrote.

1. On a desktop computer, sign in to Chrome (not an Incognito window) and click inside a text box.
2. Right-click the box and choose Talk to Type. You have to start it this way the first time and agree to the opt-in. After that you can press Option + Space on a Mac, or Ctrl + Space on Windows and ChromeOS.
3. Speak. It adds punctuation and tidies the wording. To change text you already wrote, highlight it, start Talk to Type, and say something like "make this sound more professional."
4. Select Done in the bar, or press the shortcut again, and read what it wrote.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it for emails, forms, and comments, anywhere you would otherwise type. It is desktop only, and switching tabs stops it from listening. Changing existing text is not yet available in Google Workspace apps. Chrome collects your voice recording, typed words, and page details to polish your text, so skip it for anything private.

Source: [Google Chrome Help, Use talk to type in Chrome](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/18479367?hl=en)

## Try this

Pick one message you have been putting off, like a thank-you note or a reply to a long email. Dictate it with whichever tool you already have, in under two minutes, without stopping to fix anything. Then read it once and edit the rough spots. Notice what it misheard, so you know what to check next time. Keep names, account numbers, and anything private out of it.

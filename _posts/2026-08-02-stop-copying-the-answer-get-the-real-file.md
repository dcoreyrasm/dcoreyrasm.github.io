You ask a good question and get a great answer, then spend ten minutes turning it into something useful, a document, a spreadsheet, an email you can actually send. All four tools can skip that step now and hand you the real thing directly. Here's one way to do it on each.

## Claude: create and Edit Real Files, Not Just Chat Text

![Claude: Create and Edit Real Files, Not Just Chat Text](/assets/blog/2026-08-02/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude can now build you an actual file, an Excel budget, a Word draft, a PowerPoint deck, or a PDF, ready to download instead of text stuck in a chat window.

1. In Claude.ai, go to Settings, then Capabilities, and turn on "Code execution and file creation."
2. Start a new chat and describe what you need, like "build me a moving budget spreadsheet" or "turn this into a one-page PDF."
3. Watch Claude build the file. It shows you a preview as it works.
4. Download the finished file, or save it straight to Google Drive if you've connected it.

**When to reach for it:** when what you actually need is a real spreadsheet or document to hand someone, not a paragraph you'll have to reformat yourself.

Source: [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12111783-create-and-edit-files-with-claude)

## ChatGPT: deep Research

![ChatGPT: Deep Research](/assets/blog/2026-08-02/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

Deep research has ChatGPT dig through many sources on its own and hand you back a structured, cited report instead of a quick reply.

1. Open a chat and start deep research, type "/deep research," pick it from the tools menu, or open it from the sidebar.
2. Describe exactly what you want to know. Specific beats vague.
3. Tell it which sources to use: the public web, files you upload, or apps you've connected.
4. Review the research plan it proposes, and adjust it before it starts if something's off.
5. Read the finished report when it's done, complete with citations you can check yourself.

**When to reach for it:** when you're about to make a real decision, a big purchase, a move, a health question you'll still confirm with a professional, and a quick answer isn't enough.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt)

## Copilot: researcher Agent

![Copilot: Researcher Agent](/assets/blog/2026-08-02/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Researcher is a Copilot agent built for multi-step research. Ask it something complicated and it hands back a cited, organized report instead of a quick reply.

1. Open the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, or Copilot Chat, and look for Researcher under Agents.
2. Ask your question, specific enough that it knows what you're actually deciding.
3. Tell it whether to pull from the web, your own files and messages, or both.
4. Answer any clarifying questions it asks. This is where you steer the research.
5. Read the structured report it hands back, with headings, citations, and often a chart or two.

**When to reach for it:** for a decision that deserves more than a quick chat answer, comparing real options with real trade-offs. This needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot license, or Copilot Premium inside Copilot Chat, not the free copilot.microsoft.com chat.

Source: [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-365/copilot/researcher-agent)

## Gemini: export Responses to Docs, Sheets, or Gmail

![Gemini: Export Responses to Docs, Sheets, or Gmail](/assets/blog/2026-08-02/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Once Gemini gives you an answer, you can turn it into an actual Google Doc, a Sheet, or a Gmail draft, instead of copying and pasting it yourself.

1. Ask Gemini your question and let it answer.
2. Look for "Share & export" underneath the response.
3. Choose "Export to Docs" for a document, or "Draft in Gmail" to turn it into an email.
4. If the answer includes a table, click "Export to Sheets" in the corner of that table instead.
5. Find the new Doc, draft, or Sheet waiting in the app you'd expect, no copying and pasting required.

**When to reach for it:** when Gemini gave you something good, a packing list, a comparison table, a first draft of an email, and you want it live in the app you'll actually use, not stuck in a chat window.

Source: [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14184041)

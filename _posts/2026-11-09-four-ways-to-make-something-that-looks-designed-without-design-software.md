Most of us hit the same wall. You can picture the thing, the yard sale sign, the invitation, the little graphic for the group chat, and then you open a design app and lose forty minutes to templates. The four tools below each hand you a different place to start: describe it in words, scribble it rough, hand over a list, or point at a picture that already exists. One honest note before you go. Read every word in the finished picture before you send it anywhere, and keep private details out of any tool you do not fully trust.

## Claude: claude Design

![Claude: Claude Design](/assets/blog/2026-11-09/claude.png){: .section-image }

Describe what you want and Claude builds it on a canvas next to the chat, where you can keep editing it. It is on by default on every plan, including Free.

1. In the message box, select Output, then Design. You can also just ask Claude for a design.
2. Describe what you want to build, and add context like a screenshot if you have one.
3. Review what appears on the canvas, then refine it by chatting, editing on the canvas, or leaving a comment.
4. Use the Export button in the upper right to download it, or export it as a PDF.

**When to reach for it:** reach for this when you want to keep nudging one design until it is right, instead of starting over every time. Two limits from Anthropic's own list are worth knowing before you get attached to a draft: there is no version history yet, so you cannot roll back to an earlier version, and adding a new text layer or shape by hand needs a mouse or trackpad. It also draws on the same usage limits as the rest of your work in Claude.

Source: [Anthropic Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design)

## ChatGPT: sketch

![ChatGPT: Sketch](/assets/blog/2026-11-09/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

Draw a rough doodle right inside ChatGPT and let it use your drawing as the visual guide for the finished image. OpenAI's own line is that you do not need to be a professional artist.

1. Type @Sketch in ChatGPT.
2. Draw the rough shape of what you want. OpenAI's examples are the layout of a room, the contour of an outfit, or just a funny doodle.
3. Describe the style and any other details you want on top of the drawing.
4. Send it, and ChatGPT uses your drawing as the guide for the final image.

**When to reach for it:** reach for this when the thing in your head is about arrangement, where the title sits, how the pieces are placed, and typing it out keeps landing wrong. Two things to know. Sketch arrived with Images 2.5, which OpenAI describes as rolling out across tiers, so it may not have reached your account yet. And every image made this way carries invisible watermarking and C2PA metadata, which is how a picture can later be identified as AI-made.

Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-images-2-5/)

## Copilot: create an infographic

![Copilot: Create an infographic](/assets/blog/2026-11-09/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Hand Copilot a list or a description and it lays the information out as a finished graphic. This one lives in the Microsoft Copilot app and needs a paid Microsoft 365 subscription.

1. In the Copilot app, select the Apps and more icon, then the Create icon.
2. Pick a template under Explore infographics, or write your own in the Describe the infographic you want to create box.
3. Set Color, then Infographic type: Goals and lists, Timelines, or Comparisons. Then set Size, either Portrait or Wide.
4. Select the arrow to generate it, adjust it in the Transform with AI box, then use Save and copy.

**When to reach for it:** reach for this when your content is already a list or a sequence and you want it to look organized, like a timeline for a family event or a side-by-side of three options. The boundary is stated plainly on Microsoft's page: you need an eligible Microsoft 365 subscription, so this is not one of the free Copilot features. Microsoft also notes that the app's new Home starting point is still rolling out, so what you see may not match the instructions exactly.

Source: [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/create-an-infographic-with-the-microsoft-365-copilot-app)

## Gemini: edit a picture right where you found it

![Gemini: Edit a picture right where you found it](/assets/blog/2026-11-09/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Right-click almost any image on a web page in Chrome and have Gemini rework it on the spot, without downloading the file or opening an editor.

1. On your computer, open Chrome and find an image you want to edit.
2. Right-click the image, then select Create image with Gemini.
3. In Gemini in Chrome, describe the image you want, or select one of the suggestions.
4. To keep the result, right-click it and choose Save image as, or Copy image.

**When to reach for it:** reach for this when the picture you want to start from is already on your screen. The limits here are real and all come from Google's own page: you must be 18 or over to edit images, it is not available while you are signed in to a work or school Google Account, it does not work in Incognito mode, and Google says it is still gradually releasing the feature. If Create image with Gemini does not appear for a particular image, that image may be protected or unsupported.

Source: [Google Chrome Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/16706253?hl=en)

Most of the time, the thing you're stuck on is already right there on your screen: a form you don't understand, an error message, a spreadsheet that isn't adding up. This week, four ways to point your AI straight at it instead of retyping the whole thing into a chat box. One honest catch up front: three of these only work on a Mac, and one only on Windows, so check which one actually matches your computer before you build a habit around it.

## Claude: quick Entry

![Claude: Quick Entry](/assets/blog/2026-09-30/claude.png){: .section-image }

A keyboard shortcut opens a Claude chat instantly from anywhere on your Mac, screenshot in hand.

1. Open Claude Desktop on your Mac and accept the Quick Entry prompt the first time it appears (or turn it on later in Settings > General > Desktop app).
2. From any app, double-tap the Option key to pop open a Claude chat box right where you are.
3. Click the capture icon and drag over the part of your screen you want to ask about, like a confusing chart or an error message.
4. Type your question and send. Claude answers without you switching apps, opening a browser tab, or uploading a file.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when you're already deep in something else and don't want to stop and open a new window. It's Mac only, needs Claude Desktop running in the background, and is free on every plan, including Free.

Source: [Claude Help Center: Use Quick Entry with Claude Desktop on Mac](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12626668-use-quick-entry-with-claude-desktop-on-mac)

## ChatGPT: the macOS Screenshot Tool

![ChatGPT: The macOS Screenshot Tool](/assets/blog/2026-09-30/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

Grab a picture of whatever window is open on your Mac and hand it straight to ChatGPT.

1. Open the ChatGPT app on your Mac (or its quick launcher window).
2. Click the plus icon in the message box and hover over "Take Screenshot."
3. Pick the open window you want from the list, like a spreadsheet, a webpage, or a PDF, or type the app's name to find it faster.
4. Ask your question about what's in the screenshot. No separate upload step needed.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when the thing you're confused about is already open on screen rather than saved as a file you'd have to go find. The first time, you'll need to approve screen recording access in System Settings.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center: ChatGPT macOS App Screenshot Tool](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9295245-chatgpt-macos-app-screenshot-tool)

## Copilot: take a Screenshot, Right in Copilot

![Copilot: Take a Screenshot, Right in Copilot](/assets/blog/2026-09-30/copilot.png){: .section-image }

The free Copilot app on Windows can grab part of your screen and answer questions about it on the spot.

1. Open Copilot on Windows and start typing in the composer box.
2. Select the "+" button, then choose "Take a Screenshot."
3. Drag to select the area of your screen you want Copilot to see.
4. Add your question about what's in the selection and send.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it for anything visual you'd otherwise have to describe in words, like a confusing settings menu or a form field that won't validate. This is the free consumer Copilot app on Windows, not Microsoft 365 Copilot, and it's Windows only.

Source: [Microsoft Support: Getting started with Copilot on Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/getting-started-with-copilot-on-windows)

## Gemini: speak to Window

![Gemini: Speak to Window](/assets/blog/2026-09-30/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Talk out loud into whatever you're working on, and Gemini cleans it up and drops the text right in.

1. Open the Gemini app on your Mac (macOS Sequoia 15 or later, Apple Silicon).
2. Click into the document, email, or field where you want the text to land.
3. Press and hold the fn key and start talking, then let go when you're done, or tap fn twice to keep it listening hands-free.
4. Gemini transcribes what you said and cleans up the "um"s. Highlight text first and it can also summarize or rewrite it by voice.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when typing feels slower than talking, or you want a rough draft cleaned up as you go. It's English only right now, Mac only, and still rolling out, so it may not have reached your account yet.

Source: [Gemini Apps Help: Use the Gemini app on Mac](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17011627)

## Try this

Next time you hit something confusing on your computer, a benefits enrollment form, a spreadsheet formula that won't work, a webpage you can't make sense of, skip retyping it. Screenshot it (or dictate straight into it) with whichever of these tools you already have on your computer, and ask your question right there. See how much faster you get an answer than if you'd typed the whole thing out by hand, and double check anything about money or health the same way you would if you'd looked it up yourself.

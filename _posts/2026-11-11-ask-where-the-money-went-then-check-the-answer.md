Three of the big AI tools can now link to your bank and card accounts and answer plain questions like “what did I spend eating out last month?” That’s handy right before the holidays, and it’s also the most private data you own, so this week is half how-to and half decide-first. All three are read-only, U.S.-only, and still rolling out, and the Copilot card is the work version, for a budget spreadsheet you already have. AI can sound sure and still get a number wrong, so check anything that matters against your bank before you act on it.

## Claude: personal Finance in Claude (Beta)

![Claude: Personal Finance in Claude (Beta)](/assets/blog/2026-11-11/claude.png){: .section-image }

Link your bank and card accounts once, then see balances and spending on one page and ask Claude about it in plain words.

1. Open Personal Finance in the side menu. If you don’t see it, hover over More.
2. Select Get started, pick your goals or skip them, then select Continue.
3. Select Link accounts with Plaid, choose your bank, sign in there, and pick the accounts to share.
4. Ask a real question, like “What did I spend eating out in October, and how does that compare to September?”
5. To undo it, open the three-dot menu on the Personal Finance page, choose Manage linked accounts, then Unlink.

**When to reach for it:** when you want one honest picture across several accounts. It’s read-only, so it can’t move money, and Claude never sees your bank password. It’s a beta on Pro, Max, and Ultra, U.S. banks only, and still rolling out. Balances can be a day or two old, so check big numbers with your bank.

Source: [Anthropic Help Center: Use Personal Finance in Claude](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/18185620-use-personal-finance-in-claude)

## ChatGPT: finances in ChatGPT (Preview)

![ChatGPT: Finances in ChatGPT (Preview)](/assets/blog/2026-11-11/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

Connect your accounts and ChatGPT builds a dashboard of spending, subscriptions, and upcoming payments that you can ask about.

1. Open Finances from the ChatGPT sidebar and select Get started. Or type “@Finances, connect my accounts” in any chat.
2. Sign in to your bank through Plaid, which handles the linking.
3. Give it a few minutes to sync and sort your transactions.
4. Ask something specific, like “Which subscriptions renew before the end of the year?”
5. To disconnect, go to Settings, then Apps, then Finances.

**When to reach for it:** when subscriptions and recurring charges are what you’re trying to get a grip on. When OpenAI announced it in May 2026, it was a U.S.-only preview for Pro users, with plans to bring it to Plus, so check whether you have it yet. It can’t make changes to your accounts, and OpenAI says it isn’t a replacement for professional financial advice.

Source: [OpenAI: Personal finance in ChatGPT announcement (May 2026)](https://openai.com/index/personal-finance-chatgpt/)

## Copilot: analyst in Microsoft 365 Copilot

![Copilot: Analyst in Microsoft 365 Copilot](/assets/blog/2026-11-11/copilot.png){: .section-image }

This one is the work side of money. Point Analyst at a budget spreadsheet or CSV file and it returns a report with trends, outliers, charts, and tables.

1. Sign in to microsoft365.com with your work or school account.
2. Open Microsoft Copilot, then select Analyst under Agents in the left pane.
3. Type your question, like “Which three expense categories grew the most from last quarter to this one?”
4. Select the + icon, then Attach content, and pick or upload the spreadsheet or CSV file.
5. Read the report, then spot-check two or three totals against the file yourself.

**When to reach for it:** when the money question is about a department or project budget, not your household. It needs a work or school account, and if you don’t see Analyst, your administrator may not have turned it on. It works on files you give it and doesn’t link to bank accounts, which for work data is the safer setup.

Source: [Microsoft Support: Get started with Analyst in Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://support.microsoft.com/Microsoft-365-Copilot/get-started-with-analyst-in-microsoft-365-copilot)

## Gemini: google Wallet in Gemini

![Gemini: Google Wallet in Gemini](/assets/blog/2026-11-11/gemini.png){: .section-image }

If you already use Google Wallet, Gemini can read your linked accounts and passes to summarize spending and suggest budget changes.

1. Open the Gemini app or go to gemini.google.com.
2. Ask something that needs Wallet, like “How much did I spend on groceries last month?” Add @Google Wallet if Gemini doesn’t pick it up.
3. If Wallet isn’t connected yet, Gemini offers a connect option. Follow the on-screen steps.
4. To turn it off, disconnect Google Wallet in Connected Apps at gemini.google.com/apps, and unlink bank accounts in your Google Wallet settings.

**When to reach for it:** when your cards, loyalty passes, and receipts already live in Google Wallet. You need to be 18 or older, in the U.S., on a personal Google account with Keep Activity on, and it’s still rolling out. It can’t make payments or change your linked accounts, and it isn’t available in Gems or Gemini Live.

Source: [Gemini Apps Help: Google Wallet in Gemini](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18112192)

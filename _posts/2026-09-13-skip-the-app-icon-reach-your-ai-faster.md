Every one of these four tools now has some way to skip the normal routine of unlocking your phone, finding the icon, and opening a fresh chat. A widget on your lock screen, a keyboard shortcut, a hardware key, or making the AI your phone’s actual assistant, once it’s set up, getting help takes one motion instead of four. This week’s issue walks through one shortcut per tool, and the real tradeoff that comes with each one.

## Claude: add the Claude Widget to Your Phone

![Claude: Add the Claude Widget to Your Phone](/assets/blog/2026-09-13/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude has a home screen and lock screen widget on iPhone and Android that skips the app icon entirely. Tap it and you’re straight into a new chat, dictation, or the camera.

1. On iPhone, touch and hold your Home Screen or Today View until the apps jiggle, then tap Edit in the corner.
2. Tap Add Widget, search for Claude, then tap Add Widget again and Done. (On Android, long-press your home screen, choose Widgets, and find Claude the same way.)
3. Tap the chat button to start a new conversation, the microphone to jump straight into dictation, or the camera to snap a photo for Claude to look at.
4. Grant microphone or camera permission the first time you use those buttons, since the widget needs it to work.

**When to reach for it:** good for the moment your hands are full or your phone is locked, cooking, carrying groceries, walking the dog, and you just want to ask something or jot a note without unlocking to the app icon first. Needs iOS 18 or newer, or Android 8.0 or newer, and every question through the widget still counts against your usual usage limit.

Source: [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10263469-use-claude-app-intents-shortcuts-and-widgets-on-ios)

## ChatGPT: pull Up ChatGPT With Alt+Space

![ChatGPT: Pull Up ChatGPT With Alt+Space](/assets/blog/2026-09-13/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

The ChatGPT app for Windows has a floating companion window you can summon with a keyboard shortcut, so you don’t have to alt-tab away from what you’re already working on.

1. Install the ChatGPT app for Windows from the Microsoft Store (it needs a paid plan: Plus, Team, Edu, or Enterprise).
2. Open the app once, sign in, and leave it running in the background.
3. Whenever you need it, press Alt + Space to pop open the companion window over whatever you’re doing.
4. Ask your question, upload a file, or generate an image right there, then dismiss the window and get back to your document.

**When to reach for it:** good for a quick question while you’re heads-down in a spreadsheet or document on a Windows PC and don’t want to lose your place. It needs a paid ChatGPT plan (the free plan doesn’t get the Windows app), and if Alt+Space is already used by another program, you can change the shortcut under Settings > App > Companion window hotkey.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9982051-using-the-chatgpt-windows-app)

## Copilot: press the Copilot Key (or Win+C)

![Copilot: Press the Copilot Key (or Win+C)](/assets/blog/2026-09-13/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Consumer Copilot on Windows opens instantly from a dedicated keyboard key, or Win+C if your keyboard doesn’t have one, no need to hunt for the taskbar icon.

1. Look for a Copilot key on your keyboard (common on newer Windows 11 PCs), or just remember Win+C if you don’t have one.
2. Press it to launch the Copilot app, or open a smaller quick view, depending on how it’s configured.
3. Sign in with your personal Microsoft account the first time, so your chat history and preferences carry over.
4. Go to Account > Settings > Copilot Keyboard Shortcuts if you want to change whether the key opens the full app or just the quick view.

**When to reach for it:** good for a fast fact-check or a quick draft while you’re already at your Windows PC, without switching windows or hunting the Start menu. This is the free personal copilot.microsoft.com experience tied to a Microsoft account, not the Microsoft 365 Copilot your workplace might license separately.

Source: [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/welcome-to-copilot-on-windows-675708af-8c16-4675-afeb-85a5a476ccb0)

## Gemini: make Gemini Your Phone’s Default Assistant

![Gemini: Make Gemini Your Phone’s Default Assistant](/assets/blog/2026-09-13/gemini.png){: .section-image }

On Android, you can set Gemini as your phone’s actual assistant, so the gestures and voice command you already use for Google Assistant now open Gemini instead.

1. Open the Gemini app (or download it from the Play Store) and follow the prompt to make it your assistant, or go to Settings > Apps > Default apps > Digital assistant app and choose the Google app.
2. Confirm the switch. Gemini now replaces Google Assistant as your phone’s primary assistant.
3. Use “Hey Google,” press and hold the power button, touch and hold Home, or swipe up from the bottom corner, whichever gesture you used for Google Assistant before, to bring up Gemini instead.
4. Ask your question hands-free, the same way you would have asked Google Assistant.

**When to reach for it:** good for the moments your hands are full, driving, cooking, carrying bags, when a gesture or voice command is faster than unlocking your phone and opening an app. Android only, and switching replaces Google Assistant everywhere on the phone, so it’s an either-or, not a side-by-side option.

Source: [Google Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14554984?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)

## Try this

Pick whichever of these four you actually use, and set up its shortcut this week: add the Claude widget to your phone’s home screen, switch Gemini to your default assistant, or if you’re on a Windows PC, get used to Alt+Space or the Copilot key. Then use it once for something genuinely small, checking a fact, jotting a reminder, asking what’s in the fridge, and notice whether you actually reach for it again without thinking, or whether you forget it’s there. A faster way in doesn’t mean less caution, though: an answer that took one keypress is exactly as capable of being confidently wrong as one you typed out slowly, so still check anything about money, health, or safety against a real source, and be careful what you say out loud or type into a shortcut you can trigger by accident.

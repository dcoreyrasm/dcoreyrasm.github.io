Most of us start a new chat every time, then can't find the answer we got three weeks ago. All four tools now have a way to keep one project together, a move, a renovation, a big trip, and hand the result back in a shape you'll actually use: your own voice, one workspace, or something you can listen to instead of read. Here's one way to do it on each platform.

## Claude: get answers in a style that sounds like you

![Claude: answers in a style that sounds like you](/assets/blog/2026-07-26/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude's Styles let you set how it writes back before you even ask, so a quick draft sounds like your voice instead of generic AI copy.

1. Look for "Choose style" next to the model selector in the chat window.
2. Pick one of the built-in options: Normal, Concise, Formal, or Explanatory.
3. To build your own, open Create and Edit Styles, then either upload a few things you've written so Claude learns the pattern, or just describe the tone you want.
4. Switch styles anytime, mid-conversation, depending on what you're working on.

**When to reach for it:** when you're using Claude for something that needs to sound like you, a note to a neighbor, a toast, a message you don't want to read like a form letter.

Source: [Claude Help Center](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/10181068-configuring-and-using-styles)

## ChatGPT: give one project a home instead of ten chats

![ChatGPT: give one project a home](/assets/blog/2026-07-26/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

ChatGPT Projects group your related chats, files, and instructions in one place, so you're not hunting through history for a decision you made three weeks ago.

1. Open Projects in the left-hand menu and start a new one.
2. Name it after the thing you're actually working on, like "Kitchen reno" or "Mom's 70th."
3. Add any files you already have, plus a few notes on what you want ChatGPT to remember for this project only.
4. Keep chatting inside the project. Everything you add stays together instead of scattering across your history.

**When to reach for it:** when something you're planning is going to take more than one chat, so the budget, the quotes, and the running list all live in the same place.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-using-projects-in-chatgpt)

## Copilot: turn a pile of notes into one notebook

![Copilot: turn a pile of notes into one notebook](/assets/blog/2026-07-26/copilot.png){: .section-image }

Copilot Notebooks pulls your files, links, and notes on one task into a single AI-aware workspace, then answers questions or drafts something using only that material.

1. Go to the Notebooks section at microsoft365.com, or in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app.
2. Select "All notebooks," then "New notebook," and give it a name.
3. Add your sources: upload files, pull from OneDrive, or paste in links.
4. Ask it questions grounded in just those sources, or have it draft a document from what you added.

**When to reach for it:** when you've got a scattered pile of research or quotes for one specific thing and want answers that stick to just that material. This needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot license, or a Personal, Family, or Premium Microsoft 365 subscription, not the free copilot.microsoft.com chat.

Source: [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/get-started-with-microsoft-365-copilot-notebooks)

## Gemini: turn what you've gathered into something you can listen to

![Gemini: turn it into something you can listen to](/assets/blog/2026-07-26/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Gemini's Audio Overview turns a document, a set of notes, or a Deep Research report into a podcast-style conversation you can play back instead of reading.

1. At gemini.google.com, click Add file and upload the document or notes you want turned into audio, or point it at a Deep Research report.
2. Ask Gemini to create an audio overview from it.
3. Give it a few minutes to generate. You can start a new chat while you wait, and you'll get a notification when it's ready.
4. Play it back like a podcast, while you drive, cook, or fold laundry.

**When to reach for it:** when you've already done the research and just want to absorb it a different way, on a walk or a commute, instead of rereading a document. You need to be signed in and 18 or older to use it.

Source: [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16047373)

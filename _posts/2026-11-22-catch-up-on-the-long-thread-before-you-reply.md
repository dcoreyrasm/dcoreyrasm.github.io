It's Thanksgiving week, which means at least one email thread with forty replies about who's bringing the pie. This week is one idea: let AI read the whole thread first and tell you what's been decided, what's still open, and what's on you. Then check it against the actual emails before you reply, because a summary can sound sure and still miss the one message that changed the plan. And keep threads with private details, like money or health, out of any tool you don't fully trust.

## Claude: ask Claude What's Been Decided, Right in Outlook

![Claude: Ask Claude What's Been Decided, Right in Outlook](/assets/blog/2026-11-22/claude.png){: .section-image }

Claude for Outlook is an add-in that reads a whole conversation, replies and forwards included, and tells you what's settled, what's still open, and who owes what.

1. Install the Claude for Outlook add-in using the "Get it now" link on Anthropic's help page.
2. Open Outlook, open any email, click the Claude button in the ribbon, and sign in with your Claude account.
3. Open the long thread and ask something like "What's been decided and what's still open?" or "Who owes what on this thread?"
4. Click a citation to jump to the email it came from. If you ask for a reply, it lands as an unsent draft for you to read and send yourself.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when the thread lives in Outlook and you want every answer tied to the exact email. It's in beta, needs a Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise plan, and works in Outlook on the web, Windows, and Mac, not the phone apps. Anthropic also warns that an email can hide instructions meant to steer Claude, so read every draft before it goes out.

Source: [Anthropic Help Center: Use Claude for Outlook](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14855664)

## ChatGPT: bring Your Outlook Inbox Into ChatGPT

![ChatGPT: Bring Your Outlook Inbox Into ChatGPT](/assets/blog/2026-11-22/chatgpt.png){: .section-image }

ChatGPT's Outlook Email app lets ChatGPT search your mailbox and read the messages, so you can ask about a thread from inside a chat.

1. Connect the Outlook Email app in ChatGPT and sign in with your Microsoft account.
2. Point it at the thread with a search, using from:, subject:, or a date range. For example: "Find the emails with subject: Thanksgiving from the last two weeks."
3. Ask for what you need, like "Summarize where this landed" or "Draft a reply that covers the three main points."
4. If the answer looks thin, narrow it. OpenAI says a broad search can come back incomplete, so try a shorter date range or a distinct sender or keyword.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when you already work in ChatGPT and want to ask about email without switching apps. OpenAI's page doesn't say which plans include it, and what you can connect depends on your account and any workspace settings. It can also act on email in some setups, so after an error, check Outlook before you ask it to try a send or a move again.

Source: [OpenAI Help Center: Outlook Email and Calendar apps in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12512241)

## Copilot: get the Short Version at the Top of the Thread

![Copilot: Get the Short Version at the Top of the Thread](/assets/blog/2026-11-22/copilot.png){: .section-image }

In Outlook for Microsoft 365, Copilot can scan a whole conversation and put a summary right above the emails, no prompt needed.

1. In classic Outlook or new Outlook, open the conversation you want.
2. Select Summary by Copilot at the top of the thread. It might say Summarize instead.
3. Read the summary at the top of the email. If it shows numbered citations, select one to jump to that message.
4. In new Outlook, if there's a PDF, Word, or PowerPoint attachment, select Summarize a file to get the gist of that too.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when you want a quick read without leaving the thread or typing anything. It's part of Outlook for Microsoft 365 with Copilot, and Microsoft's page doesn't spell out which plans include it. Citations aren't promised every time, so open the original email before you act on a date or a dollar amount.

Source: [Microsoft Support: Summarize an email thread with Copilot in Outlook](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/summarize-an-email-thread-with-copilot-in-outlook)

## Gemini: summarize the Thread, Then Set a Reminder in Gmail

![Gemini: Summarize the Thread, Then Set a Reminder in Gmail](/assets/blog/2026-11-22/gemini.png){: .section-image }

Gmail's AI Overview can summarize a long thread, and when the thread asks something of you by a date, it can turn that into a reminder.

1. Make sure smart features are turned on in your Gmail settings.
2. On your computer, go to Gmail and open the email thread.
3. At the top of the thread, click Summarize this email.
4. If the overview shows a task with a deadline for you, click Remind me next to it. Use Edit reminder to change the date.

**When to reach for it:** reach for it when the thread is in Gmail and you don't want to install anything. Google says it's available to all Gmail users, but only in some languages, and the steps cover Gmail on a computer. Turning smart features off also turns off other AI writing help in Gmail, and Google says not to rely on Gemini features for medical, legal, or financial advice.

Source: [Gmail Help: Catch up on email threads with AI Overview conversation summaries](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16561387)

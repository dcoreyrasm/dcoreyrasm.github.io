Why It Matters

Training alone isn't enough. Neither is a policy alone.

Many organizations start using AI before they have shared guidance. Some staff experiment freely, others avoid it entirely, and leaders aren't sure what's happening. Training helps people build skills, but without clear expectations, everyone fills in the gaps differently.

Organizations need both: practical training so people can use AI well, and clear expectations about what's appropriate, what needs review, and who decides.

Organizational AI governance is a long-standing professional interest of mine and the focus of my ongoing doctoral research at Fairfield University. That research is still in progress. In my consulting, I draw on practical experience and published guidance, and I'm clear about the difference between what we know and what is still being studied.

What I Can Provide

Services shaped to your scope.

  • Review of organizational workflows to see where AI is already in use and where it could help.
  • AI opportunity and risk assessment covering benefits, privacy concerns, and areas that need human review.
  • Tool and vendor evaluation support so you can compare options against your needs.
  • Responsible AI use guidelines written in plain language your staff can follow.
  • Staff training and leadership workshops that connect the guidelines to everyday work.
  • Implementation recommendations for roles, review steps, and communication.
  • A 30 to 60-day action plan with clear priorities and owners.

Scope

What this service is, and what it isn't.

This is practical consulting to help your organization adopt AI thoughtfully. It does not include legal advice, regulatory or compliance certification, or cybersecurity assessments or certification. When those questions come up, I'll recommend you work with your legal counsel or a qualified specialist.

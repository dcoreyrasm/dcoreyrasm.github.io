Nova

Every techie has an origin story. This one belongs to Nova, and part of it is mine.

Nova and the Commodore 64 is an animated series I’ve been creating with AI. In the story, Nova gets a Commodore 64 from her parents and teaches herself BASIC, one line at a time.

The series isn’t perfect yet. Keeping a character looking the same from one AI-generated scene to the next takes real work, and I’m learning as I go. It’s a small project I pick up when work and school have my brain overloaded. Writing prompts for animation has also been a different kind of learning for me.

My goal is simple. I want girls to see themselves in the story of technology early, before anyone tells them it isn’t for them.

Here’s a first look at Episode 1. You’ll notice some details aren’t quite right yet, and that’s part of the experiment. I’d love to hear what you think.

November 25, 2026

Brave & Bright: Girls in STEM

I’m so happy to share that Brave & Bright: Girls in STEM is now available.

I created this coloring book to help girls see themselves in science, technology, engineering, and math. It has 29 designs of girls building robots, coding, looking through microscopes, and exploring space. Encouraging messages throughout the book help build curiosity and confidence. It’s written for ages 6 to 12.

My own start in tech came as a child, when my parents bought a Commodore 64. I loved that computer and spent hours typing BASIC programs just to watch a sprite move across the screen. That’s where my curiosity about technology began. My Atari helped too. I’ll never forget the sounds of Pac-Man and Space Invaders coming from the TV for three or four days straight during one winter break.

In college at Fairfield University, I worked in the computer labs and taught myself much of what I know. I didn’t always see people who looked like me in this field. I want girls to see themselves here early and to hear messages like these:

  • I belong in STEM.
  • My questions matter.
  • I can build amazing things.

If you have a curious girl in your life, or you know a teacher, librarian, or program that supports girls in STEM, I’d love for you to pass this along.

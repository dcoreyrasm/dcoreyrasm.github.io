Why This Exists

Most of this money and help never reaches the people it is meant for.

Written for the organization that does not have a grant writer

Program pages are written for institutions with development staff. Each entry here is restated in plain language: what you actually get, who can actually apply, and what the first step is if you decide to pursue it.

The catch, said out loud

“Free” often means discounted, credits that expire into a bill, or a tool you cannot leave once your data is inside it. Where that is true, the entry says so under “Watch out for” rather than leaving you to find out after you have committed.

Three ways in, named honestly

A good share of this list cannot be applied to cold at all — access runs through an employee, a partner, or a nomination. Hiding that wastes your afternoon. Those are listed anyway, marked by introduction, so you know they exist and how introductions actually happen.

Checked, dated, and curated by one person

Every entry was read against its official source and carries the date it was last checked. Nothing is auto-scraped into this list. Programs still change their terms without notice, so treat the official page as the authority and this as the map to it.

How to read an entry. “Watch out for” is the honest caveat — a program that is discounted rather than free, credits that expire into a bill, or a funder you cannot approach cold. “Suggested next step” is the concrete thing to do if you want to pursue it.

Verify before you apply. Programs change their terms, cycles, and pricing without notice. Each entry links to its official source and shows the date it was last checked — treat that source, not this page, as the authority.

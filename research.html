Doctoral Research

Practitioner. Scholar. Both at once.

As an Executive DBA candidate at Fairfield University (expected 2028), I'm researching AI governance in higher education. Universities are working out how to govern a technology that is already part of everyday work, and I deal with that same question in my role at Yale.

My Research Area

AI governance in higher education.

I’ll share my research question and findings once the work is further along.

Why AI Governance?

Universities have long experience governing technology, but AI raises new questions about risk, responsibility, and who decides how it’s used. Many of those questions don’t have settled answers yet. I think they deserve careful study, not only quick policy responses.

Why Higher Education?

A university brings teaching, research, and administration together under one roof, and each of those areas meets AI in its own way. That mix makes higher education a useful place to understand how organizations make decisions about a technology that is still changing.

Why Practitioner Insight Matters Here

I’m not studying this from the outside. I’ve spent 19 years at Yale, 14 of them in director-level and senior leadership roles, and my work now includes designing and leading AI workshops and co-leading AI support and governance work. That experience shapes the questions I ask and keeps the research tied to what actually happens inside a complex institution.

How Research and Practice Reinforce Each Other

My work at Yale gives my research its context, and the research gives me a more careful way to think about the AI support and governance work I co-lead. Moving between practice and theory is what the practitioner-scholar model is built on.

The Connection

Research, leadership, and strategy as a loop.

What I study at Fairfield, I test at Yale, and what I build at Yale, I examine through research. That cycle is intentional, and it's what keeps both the scholarship and the practice honest.

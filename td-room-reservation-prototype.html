TD Room Reservation System

A clickable prototype for staff review, built from the room workflow document.

The workflow document describes the same eight steps for every space. What actually differs between rooms is three things: whether training is required, whether the room uses swipe access, and whether long-term bookings are allowed. This prototype treats those as settings on a Rooms table rather than five separate workflows, and runs the intake checks live so you can see what the automation would decide.

What the workflow document actually says differs

Read across the five spaces, the eight steps are identical. These are the only real differences.

  • Student Kitchen — training required, swipe access, no long-term bookings. Training incomplete holds the booking in PENDING rather than denying it outright.
  • Thompson Room — no training, no swipe or key access needed, no long-term bookings. The simplest space.
  • Student Lounge — no training, swipe access, no long-term bookings. Swipe is revoked automatically after the event.
  • Art Studio — training required, swipe access, and it is the one space that does allow long-term bookings.
  • Recording Studio — the section is empty in the document. It reads "[Jake]" and nothing else, so none of its three rules are known.

One other thing worth confirming: the Art Studio section says the student "fills out a reservation form for the Lounge." That reads like a copy-paste carryover from the Lounge section rather than a real rule, so this prototype treats it as the Art Studio form.

Proposed Airtable structure

Seven tables. The per-room differences live as fields on Rooms, which is what keeps this one workflow instead of five.

Automations

The workflow document names one automation, "Send Booking Confirmation." Getting the described behavior takes seven, because scheduled actions and denial notices cannot ride on the same trigger.
